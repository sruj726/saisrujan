## Top 5 (Opportunity Score /100)
1. **SubShield** — COI tracking for contractors — **78.0**
2. **CredCadence** — provider credentialing & CAQH re-attestation — **75.5**
3. **FleetFile** — DOT driver qualification files — **75.0**
4. **LicenseLedger** — trade license/cert tracking — **72.5** (better as SubShield module)
5. **InspectLoop** — fire & life-safety inspections — **71.0** (needs domain expertise)

## The pattern that wins
**An expiring document + a third party who must supply it + a real penalty when it lapses + a named person whose job is preventing that lapse.**
Everything that lacked one of these (marketing tools, horizontal CRMs, features that platforms bundle for free) scored at the bottom.

## Final answer: $1,000, 30 days, one developer
**I would build SubShield (COI tracking for small/mid commercial contractors).** Not because it's the biggest market, but because it has the best combination of:

1. **A hard-dollar ROI the buyer has already felt.** Premium-audit chargebacks for uninsured subs are a real bill, often four to five figures. You aren't selling "productivity"; you're selling "never pay that again."
2. **A standardized input that makes AI genuinely useful.** The ACORD 25 is one form used industry-wide. Extraction is reliable, cheap (cents per certificate), and the main labor being replaced.
3. **A buyer you can find and reach.** GCs are listed publicly; the office manager/controller owns this job; brokers and CFMA/AGC chapters are natural channels.
4. **A concierge version that sells before the code exists.** The "free COI audit → paid pilot" funnel validates with money in under 3 weeks, and the audit tooling becomes the product.
5. **Retention gets stronger with age.** Audits look backward, so the history becomes the moat, and archive mode keeps even churned customers paying a little.
6. **Low data sensitivity** compared with credentialing (SSNs) or DOT files (driver medical/PII), so a 2-person team can secure it properly.
7. **Clear expansion** into licenses, W-9s, lien waivers, property managers, and broker white-label, all on the same engine.

**Why not the others:**
- **CredCadence** is a close second with higher willingness to pay, but sensitive PII raises the security bar from day one, and practice managers are hard to reach. It's my fallback if SubShield fails validation.
- **FleetFile** has easy targeting (daily new-authority lists), but tiny carriers churn hard and ELD vendors can bundle it.
- **InspectLoop** is too big a build for 30 days.

**What would change my mind:** if after ~25 real GC conversations fewer than 2 will pay for a pilot, or most say "our broker already does this for free," stop and run the same paid-pilot test on CredCadence. **Money collected, not enthusiasm, makes the decision.**
