# Part 1 — 30 Boring Subscription Ideas (Ideas 1–10)

> Read this with a skeptic's eye. In boring B2B software, the code is maybe 20% of the outcome; **distribution, trust, and data that becomes painful to leave** are the other 80%. Each idea below is judged on that, not just on whether it can be built.
>
> Competitor names reflect the market as I understand it up to my knowledge cutoff. Check current pricing and features before you rely on any comparison.

Legend for "Verdict": **BUILD-CANDIDATE**, **CONDITIONAL** (works only with a specific niche/edge), **REJECT**.

## 1. SubShield — Certificate-of-Insurance (COI) tracking for contractors — **BUILD-CANDIDATE**

1. **Product name:** SubShield (alts: CertChase, COI Pilot)
2. **Concept:** Forward or upload subcontractor certificates of insurance; AI reads them, checks them against your requirements, and automatically chases subs and their insurance agents before anything expires.
3. **Exact customer:** Commercial general contractors and specialty contractors with $3M–$75M revenue and 20–300 active subs/vendors. The buyer is the office manager, project accountant, controller, or risk manager.
4. **Recurring problem:** Every sub needs a valid COI (GL, auto, workers' comp, umbrella, additional insured, waiver of subrogation) before starting work and before each payment. Policies renew annually on staggered dates, so some certificates expire **every single week**.
5. **Current solution:** Excel or Google Sheets, an Outlook folder, calendar reminders, custom fields in Sage/Foundation/QuickBooks, Procore's basic insurance fields, or a pricey enterprise platform.
6. **Why it's annoying:** Staff read ACORD 25 forms by hand, check limits and endorsement boxes, chase agents by email, and often find lapses only during the insurer's annual **premium audit**.
7. **Why pay monthly:** At a premium audit, the GC's insurer charges premium on the payroll/cost of any sub who can't prove coverage. That's often **$5k–$50k+**. Claims involving uninsured subs also hit the GC's own policy. The monitoring never ends.
8. **Price:** $99 (≤50 active vendors) / $199 (≤200) / $399 (≤600). Done-for-you add-on: +$250/mo.
9. **Customer value:** 5–15 admin hours/month ($200–$600) plus avoiding a single audit chargeback per year ($5k–$50k). ROI is easy to defend.
10. **MVP features:** vendor CSV import; requirement templates by trade; a dedicated email-in address plus a no-login upload link; AI extraction of ACORD 25 fields with a human confirm screen; compliance status (compliant/deficient/expiring/expired/missing); automated request and reminder emails to sub and agent; 30/14/7-day expiry reminders; weekly digest; audit-ready PDF/CSV export.
11. **NOT initially:** full endorsement-document parsing, Procore/Sage/ERP integrations, a mobile app, lien waivers, prequalification questionnaires, SSO, payment holds, multi-entity.
12. **Workflow:** import vendors → pick requirement template → system emails subs/agents → COIs arrive by email or link → AI extracts → admin confirms in about 30 seconds → status updates → auto-reminders before expiry → weekly report → audit export.
13. **Integrations/APIs:** inbound email parsing (Postmark/SendGrid/Mailgun inbound), transactional email, a vision-capable LLM API plus PDF text extraction, S3/R2 storage, Stripe. Later: QuickBooks, Procore, Sage Intacct, Microsoft 365.
14. **Is AI necessary?** **Yes, moderately.** Reading certificates is the core labor being replaced. ACORD 25 is a standardized form, so extraction is very reliable, but keep a human-confirm step.
15. **Build difficulty:** 4/10 for the MVP (CRUD + email + extraction + a scheduler).
16. **Competitors:** myCOI, TrustLayer, Jones, Billy, BCS, PINS Advantage, SmartCompliance, Evident, Certfocus, Constrafor, plus Procore's built-in fields.
17. **Competitor gaps:** Many sell through demos and annual contracts and price for mid-market and enterprise. Some are services-heavy. Procore's tracking is manual. QuickBooks-level GCs with 30–200 subs are underserved by self-serve, transparently priced tools.
18. **Differentiation:** self-serve setup in an afternoon; public monthly pricing; "forward COIs to coi@yourco.subshield.io"; agent-friendly no-login upload; a **premium-audit-ready report** as the hero feature; pricing by active vendor.
19. **Acquisition:** outbound to GCs (state license databases, AGC/ABC/CFMA member directories); **construction insurance brokers as a referral channel** (they spend unpaid hours fixing their clients' audit messes); SEO for "COI tracking spreadsheet"; a free "COI checker" tool.
20. **Best first niche:** commercial GCs with $5M–$50M revenue in one or two Sun Belt metros (TX/FL/GA/NC/AZ).
21. **First 10:** 300-company list → cold email + phone → offer a free 48-hour "COI audit" of their current spreadsheet/folder → convert to a $299 paid pilot. Ask 5 construction brokers for 2 intros each.
22. **First 100:** broker referral program (20% recurring), CFMA/AGC chapter lunch-and-learns, SEO templates, Procore/QuickBooks marketplace listings, then expand to commercial property managers.
23. **Churn risk:** Low to medium. Roughly 2–4% monthly, lower on $199+ plans.
24. **Why they'd cancel:** they adopt an ERP/Procore module, a broker offers free tracking, the champion admin leaves, the company shrinks, or the product goes unused because nobody set up the inbox forwarding.
25. **Retention features:** historical audit archive (insurers audit back years); sub/agent portal with saved profiles; AP "do not pay" flags; monthly "exposure avoided" report; involving PMs.
26. **Upsells:** done-for-you chasing ($250–$500/mo); lien waivers; W-9 + license tracking; sub prequal; extra entities; broker white-label.
27. **Operating costs:** hosting $50–150/mo, email $20–50/mo, LLM about $0.01–0.05 per certificate, storage negligible. Total about 3–6% of revenue plus Stripe fees.
28. **Profit margin:** 85–90% gross; 60–75% net while you're a solo founder.
29. **Legal/privacy/security:** a COI is informational, not proof of coverage, so you need a clear disclaimer; extraction errors (require confirmation; never imply guarantees); you are not giving insurance advice; business data, low PII; SOC 2 will be asked for by larger GCs later; carry your own E&O policy.
30. **Long-term expansion:** a full vendor-compliance platform (licenses, W-9s, safety records, lien waivers, prequal), property management and franchise verticals, payment holds embedded in AP, broker white-label, embedded insurance referrals.

## 2. FleetFile — DOT Driver Qualification (DQ) files for small fleets — **BUILD-CANDIDATE**

1. **Name:** FleetFile (alts: DQ Ready, AuditReady Fleet)
2. **Concept:** Digital driver qualification files for small trucking fleets. It tracks every FMCSA-required document and expiration, collects driver documents by text message, and keeps you audit-ready.
3. **Customer:** Motor carriers with 3–75 power units, plus CDL-running construction, waste, and bus operators. The buyer is the owner or safety manager.
4. **Problem:** 49 CFR Part 391 requires a DQ file per driver: application, MVRs, annual review, medical examiner's certificate (expires ≤2 years, often 1), road test/CDL, previous employer inquiries, and annual Clearinghouse queries. Something is always expiring, and new carriers face a **new-entrant safety audit within their first 12 months**.
5. **Current:** paper binders, spreadsheets, safety consultants ($100–$500/mo), J.J. Keller Encompass, Foley, ELD-vendor compliance modules.
6. **Annoying:** paper chaos; an expired med card means the driver is unqualified (out-of-service risk, liability); audit violations bring civil penalties and safety-rating damage; consultants are slow.
7. **Pay monthly:** continuous expirations, driver turnover (very high in trucking), and insurance underwriters asking for proof.
8. **Price:** $49 base + $8/active driver (typically $89–$399).
9. **Value:** avoided penalties and conditional ratings (which raise insurance cost and lose broker loads), 4–8 hrs/mo saved, faster driver onboarding.
10. **MVP:** driver roster; a 391-based checklist per driver; SMS upload link (driver photographs med card/CDL); AI date extraction; expiry alerts; annual review task with e-sign; FMCSA-compliant driver application form; one-click audit-ready PDF DQ file.
11. **NOT initially:** MVR ordering, Clearinghouse integration, drug & alcohol consortium, ELD/HOS, IFTA, vehicle maintenance files.
12. **Workflow:** add drivers → drivers get a text to upload docs → AI reads dates → checklist shows gaps → 60/30/7-day alerts → annual tasks → export at audit.
13. **Integrations:** Twilio SMS, LLM extraction, e-signature (Dropbox Sign/Documenso), FMCSA public carrier data (SAFER/QCMobile API), Stripe. Later: MVR vendors, D&A consortiums.
14. **AI necessary?** Helpful, not essential (date extraction from photos).
15. **Difficulty:** 5/10. Simple tech, but regulatory accuracy must be right.
16. **Competitors:** J.J. Keller Encompass, Foley, Tenstreet, DriverReach, Motive and Samsara compliance features, Fleetworthy, local safety consultants.
17. **Gaps:** Tenstreet/DriverReach are recruiting-first and mid-market. J.J. Keller is broad, complex, and pricey for tiny fleets. ELD modules are shallow. Tiny carriers stay on paper.
18. **Differentiate:** SMS-first and Spanish-language driver experience; "audit-ready in a weekend"; plain pricing; a DQ compliance score.
19. **Acquisition:** **the FMCSA Register publishes new authority applicants daily**, a perfect trigger list of new entrants. Also trucking insurance agents, factoring companies, and trucking YouTube/Facebook groups.
20. **Best niche:** new-entrant carriers (0–18 months authority) with 2–15 trucks.
21. **First 10:** call new authority registrants; sell "New Entrant Audit Prep" ($199 setup + $49/mo).
22. **First 100:** trucking insurance agents and factoring companies as referral partners; DOT-audit content on YouTube; safety consultants as resellers.
23. **Churn:** medium-high for tiny carriers (5–7%/mo; many go out of business in freight downturns), low for 10+ trucks.
24. **Cancel reasons:** carrier closes; bundled ELD vendor features; a consultant takes over; "the audit is over."
25. **Retention:** Clearinghouse annual reminders; maintenance file module; CSA score monitoring; 3-year retention archive.
26. **Upsells:** MVR pulls (markup), D&A consortium referral, vehicle files, done-for-you DQ build ($299), IFTA.
27. **Costs:** SMS about $0.01/message, hosting, storage. Very low.
28. **Margin:** 80–88%.
29. **Legal:** heavy driver PII (DL, DOB, medical cert, possibly SSN), so encryption and access controls are mandatory; FCRA if you pull MVRs/background (partner with a consumer reporting agency); not legal advice; DQ retention rules (keep for employment + 3 years).
30. **Expansion:** small-fleet safety OS (maintenance, D&A, CSA), carrier-vetting data for brokers, insurance-data partnerships.

## 3. CredCadence — provider credentialing & re-attestation tracker for small practices — **BUILD-CANDIDATE**

1. **Name:** CredCadence (alts: CredKeeper, PanelGuard)
2. **Concept:** Tracks every clinician's license, DEA, board certification, malpractice policy, CAQH re-attestation, payer revalidation, and monthly exclusion screening, and nags the right person before anything lapses.
3. **Customer:** independent behavioral health, medical, PT/OT, and dental groups with 3–40 clinicians, plus small credentialing/billing companies that manage many practices.
4. **Problem:** CAQH ProView requires **re-attestation every 120 days**. State licenses renew on 1–3 year cycles, DEA every 3 years, Medicare revalidation every 5, plus malpractice renewals and monthly OIG/SAM exclusion checks. A lapse means denied claims.
5. **Current:** spreadsheets, office-manager memory, outsourced credentialing firms ($100–$300/provider/month or per-application fees), or enterprise tools.
6. **Annoying:** denials discovered 30–60 days later; a dozen payer portals; knowledge walks out the door when the office manager leaves.
7. **Pay monthly:** one lapsed enrollment can delay or deny **$10k–$40k** of a clinician's billings.
8. **Price:** $25–$40/provider/month (min $99); credentialing-firm tier $299+.
9. **Value:** large in revenue protection, plus 5–10 admin hours/month.
10. **MVP:** provider profiles pre-filled from the **NPPES NPI Registry API**; document vault with AI expiry extraction; a renewal engine (license/DEA/board/malpractice/CAQH 120-day/payer revalidation); tasks and reminders to provider + admin; **monthly OIG LEIE + SAM.gov exclusion screening** with a saved audit log; dashboard.
11. **NOT initially:** automated primary-source verification, payer enrollment form filling, a CAQH integration, NCQA/CVO claims, privileging.
12. **Workflow:** add providers (NPI lookup) → upload docs → AI extracts dates → add payer enrollments → engine schedules tasks → admin completes → monthly exclusion screen → audit log.
13. **Integrations:** NPPES API, OIG LEIE monthly file, SAM.gov exclusions API, email/SMS, LLM, e-sign. Later: Nursys e-Notify, state board lookups.
14. **AI?** Not essential. Extraction helps; the rules engine matters more.
15. **Difficulty:** 5–6/10.
16. **Competitors:** Medallion, Verifiable, CredentialStream (HealthStream), symplr, Modio Health, Andros, Certify/ProviderTrust (exclusion screening), human credentialing firms.
17. **Gaps:** venture-backed players are moving upmarket to health systems and networks. Small practices get spreadsheets or human outsourcers.
18. **Differentiate:** made for 3–40 clinician groups and freelance credentialers; exclusion screening included; a 120-day CAQH cadence engine; a multi-client view for credentialing firms.
19. **Acquisition:** medical billing companies and freelance credentialers (resell/multi-client), group-practice owner communities, SEO ("CAQH re-attestation reminder").
20. **Best niche:** group behavioral-health/therapy practices with 5–30 clinicians (high clinician turnover, many payer panels, low clinical PHI in the product).
21. **First 10:** practice-owner Facebook/LinkedIn groups plus a free "credentialing health check."
22. **First 100:** a billing-company and credentialing-freelancer channel, plus content.
23. **Churn:** low (2–3%/mo).
24. **Cancel:** acquisition/consolidation, outsourcing, EHR adding the feature.
25. **Retention:** exclusion-screening audit trail (needed for compliance), provider self-service portal, payer enrollment status board.
26. **Upsells:** done-for-you credentialing (high margin as a service), payer enrollment workflows, PSV.
27. **Costs:** very low.
28. **Margin:** 85%+ software; 40–60% for any service layer.
29. **Legal:** provider PII (SSN, DOB, DEA numbers) needs strong encryption and audit logs. Mostly not PHI, but customers will ask about HIPAA/BAA, so be ready to sign one. Don't claim PSV or NCQA compliance without doing it.
30. **Expansion:** payer enrollment automation, CVO, privileging for ASCs, multi-state telehealth licensing.

## 4. LicenseLedger — workforce license & certification tracker for trades — **CONDITIONAL** (best as a module of #1)

12. **Workflow:** import → SMS links → AI read → dashboard → reminders → share packets.
13. **Integrations:** Twilio, LLM, storage; later ServiceTitan, Gusto, Procore.
14. **AI?** Helpful only.
15. **Difficulty:** 3–4/10.
16. **Competitors:** Expiration Reminder, SafetyCulture training, Procore certifications, Connecteam, BambooHR/Rippling custom fields, Salus.
17. **Gaps:** generic; no trade-specific rules; poor field UX.
18. **Differentiate:** trade-specific rules library, SMS-first, QR card.

23. **Churn:** medium (3–4%).
24. **Cancel:** "nice to have", HRIS covers it.
25. **Retention:** CE tracker, prequal packet generator.
26. **Upsells:** **COI tracking (#1)**, permits, training partners.
27. **Costs:** very low.
28. **Margin:** 85–90%.
29. **Legal:** employee PII (license numbers, DOB).
30. **Expansion:** contractor compliance hub. **Critical note:** on its own the pain is moderate. It's a stronger feature of SubShield than a standalone company.

## 5. InspectLoop — recurring inspections & deficiency follow-up for fire/life-safety contractors — **CONDITIONAL** (domain expertise required)

1. **Name:** InspectLoop
2. **Concept:** Schedules recurring NFPA inspections, captures mobile inspection reports, turns deficiencies into quotes, and reminds building owners. Built for small fire-protection companies.
3. **Customer:** fire sprinkler/alarm/extinguisher/hood/backflow contractors with 2–25 technicians.
4. **Problem:** every building has monthly/quarterly/semiannual/annual inspection obligations; reports go to owners and often to AHJ portals; deficiencies should become repair revenue but leak.
5. **Current:** paper forms, fillable PDFs, generic field service tools, or ServiceTrade/Inspect Point/BuildOps/Uptick.
6. **Annoying:** retyping reports; missed recurring visits (lost revenue); deficiencies never quoted.
7. **Pay monthly:** it runs their core revenue loop.
8. **Price:** $199–$599/mo or $59/tech.
9. **Value:** recovered deficiency revenue (often the most profitable work) and retention of recurring contracts.
10. **MVP:** customers/buildings/systems; recurrence engine; offline mobile checklists for 2–3 inspection types; PDF reports; deficiency → quote; owner reminders.
11. **NOT:** dispatch optimization, accounting, AHJ portal APIs, all NFPA forms.
12. **Workflow:** schedule generated → tech inspects on phone → PDF report auto-sent → deficiencies auto-quoted → follow-ups.
13. **Integrations:** QuickBooks, email/SMS, PDF generation, offline-capable mobile/PWA.
14. **AI?** Not necessary (maybe voice-to-deficiency notes later).
15. **Difficulty:** 7/10 (offline mobile, many forms, scheduling edge cases).
16. **Competitors:** ServiceTrade, Inspect Point, BuildOps, Uptick, ServiceTitan, Jobber + paper.
17. **Gaps:** pricing and complexity for 2–5 tech shops; generic FSMs lack NFPA forms.
18. **Differentiate:** "deficiency revenue recovery" positioning; fast setup; a niche (e.g., extinguisher + hood companies).
19. **Acquisition:** NFSA/AFSA chapters, state fire-marshal association events, distributors.
20. **Niche:** extinguisher/kitchen-hood suppression companies (simpler forms, high visit volume).
21. **First 10:** ride-alongs and phone calls; must speak their language.
22. **First 100:** distributor partnerships; association events.
23. **Churn:** low once adopted (system of record).
24. **Cancel:** acquisition by a PE roll-up (very common in fire & life safety now) that standardizes on ServiceTrade/BuildOps.
25. **Retention:** building history, deficiency pipeline.
26. **Upsells:** customer portal, payments, AHJ submission service.
27. **Costs:** low infra, **high support/onboarding**.
28. **Margin:** 75–85%.
29. **Legal:** inspection reports are life-safety records, so liability flows through the contractor; you need data integrity and immutable records.
30. **Expansion:** full vertical FSM. **Critical note:** strong market, but it's a 6-month build and a support-heavy sale. Not founder-friendly without industry experience.

16. **Competitors:** Avalara MyLodgeTax, Granicus Host Compliance (sells to cities), local consultants.
17. **Gaps:** nobody owns operator-side permit tracking well.
18. **Differentiate:** curated jurisdiction data for top markets.
19. **Acquisition:** PMS marketplaces, VRMA, STR Facebook groups.
20. **Niche:** managers in 1–2 heavily regulated states.
